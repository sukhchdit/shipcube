# ADR-0010: Carrier adapter layer, resilience, rate-shopping fan-out and raw payload retention

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Deciders:** principal-architect
- **Related:** brief §4.3, §4.6, Appendix B; REQ-CARR-001..021, REQ-QUOTE-008, REQ-API-017/018, DR-B15, DR-B16, DR-B29, DR-B30, DR-B32; D-003, D-012, D-021; NFR-PERF-002/003, NFR-RES-001..003/006, NFR-QUAL-005; ADR-0011 (third-party account rules), ADR-0021 (object storage)

## Context

One interface, many adapters; nothing outside `packages/carriers` knows a wire format. Adapters: DHL Express (MyDHL API), FedEx REST, UPS REST (OAuth), deterministic Mock. Resilience: per-call timeout, retry with backoff + jitter on idempotent operations only, one circuit breaker per carrier account, bulkheads. Rate shopping fans out in parallel with per-carrier deadlines, returns partial results, streams via SSE, and caches identical requests briefly in Redis. Raw requests/responses (redacted) are kept in object storage. No credentials exist in this run (D-003): adapters are built from current docs with recorded/synthesised `nock` fixtures and the Mock carries e2e.

**Values:** REQ-CARR-017/DR-B32 (rates 4 s, tracking 10 s, cancel 10 s, pickup 15 s, create 20 s; ≤ 2 retries; breaker 50 % over the last 20 calls, min 10, or 5 consecutive failures; bulkhead 10 per account) are the defaults. NFR-RES-001..003 originally stated different numbers; product-manager aligned them to the REQ values in D-035(f) and D-ARCH-004 records the same choice. All are configuration keys.

## Decision

### Interface and context

`CarrierAdapter` exactly as brief §4.3 (`code`, `capabilities()`, `getRates`, `createShipment`, `cancelShipment`, `schedulePickup`, `cancelPickup`, `track`, optional `validateAddress`, `findServicePoints`, `uploadCustomsDocuments`, `estimateLandedCost`; *plus the optional `recoverLabel` added 2026-11-12 by amendment 11 item 3, under A-057's "architect may add an optional operation"*; *`getRates` returns `RateResponse { quotes, noServiceReason? }` instead of a bare `RateQuote[]`, amendment 12 item 1, 2026-11-20*). `CarrierContext = { account: CarrierAccountRef, environment: 'sandbox' | 'production', correlationId, mockScenario?, locale, deadlineAt }`. `capabilities()` returns the REQ-CARR-002 declaration (per-service limits, divisors, cut-offs, label formats, push tracking, idempotent-create flag, max tracking numbers per call). Optional operations not declared → `CAPABILITY_NOT_SUPPORTED` without network I/O (REQ-CARR-003) — *extended 2026-11-12 by amendment 11 item 2 to `cancelShipment`, which an adapter may declare unsupported (DHL Express)*. All inputs/outputs are canonical Platform types (`packages/shared`; *for now the vocabularies are in `packages/carriers` and move with TASK-080, amendment 12 item 2, 2026-11-20*): services (DR-B30 `EXPRESS|STANDARD|ECONOMY` tiers + `<CARRIER>_<code>`), surcharges, errors, tracking statuses (DR-B18). Unmapped services are dropped and logged; unmapped surcharges → `CARRIER_OTHER` with the original label in `raw`; unmapped errors → `CARRIER_UNKNOWN_ERROR`.

### Ports injected into adapters (no direct I/O)

`HttpClient` (the single SSRF-safe outbound client, ADR-0016/REQ-SEC-010: https only, host allow-list per environment, private-IP and redirect checks, timeout, size cap), `TokenCache` (Redis; OAuth tokens cached until 60 s before expiry; single in-flight refresh per account; 401 → drop + one retry, REQ-CARR-016), `SecretResolver` (resolves `secretRef` → value at call time from env/Secrets Manager; values never logged — Pino redaction keys), `Clock`, `Logger`.

### Resilience wrapper (`CarrierGateway`, one per carrier **account**)

Order: bulkhead → breaker → timeout → retry → adapter call.

- **Timeouts** via `AbortSignal.timeout`: rates 4 000 ms, track 10 000, cancel 10 000, pickup 15 000, create 20 000 (DR-B32). Token fetch 5 000.
- **Retries** (`p-retry`-style in-house, seeded RNG for tests): max 2, base 200 ms × 2, full jitter, on network error / 429 / 502 / 503 / 504, **only** for `getRates`, `track`, `findServicePoints`, `validateAddress`, `cancelShipment`, `cancelPickup`. Never for `createShipment`/`schedulePickup` (REQ-CARR-017); after an ambiguous `createShipment` (timeout/5xx) the saga marks the shipment `EXCEPTION` unless the adapter declares idempotent create by client reference (DR-B04 G2).
- **Circuit breaker** `opossum` 10 per account: opens at 50 % failures over the last 20 calls (min 10) or 5 consecutive failures; half-open after 30 s with one probe; open → fail fast ≤ 50 ms with `CARRIER_UNAVAILABLE`. Breaker state is a gauge metric and feeds `GET /carriers/status`.
- **Bulkhead** `p-limit(10)` per account (concurrent calls); queue wait beyond the operation deadline → `CARRIER_BUSY` — *superseded 2026-11-12 by amendment 1: two pools per account, `rates` 6 and `transactional` 4, with the anonymous budget and quota guard of amendments 2–3*.

### Rate shopping (`quotes` module → `RateShopper`)

Eligible accounts = enabled accounts whose declared services match direction/content type/weight/dimensions (DR-B34). Fan-out with `Promise.allSettled` under a per-carrier deadline 4 000 ms and overall 5 000 ms (DR-B16); late responses are discarded and not cached. Each carrier result is priced by `PricingService` (ADR-0015) and emitted as an SSE `carrier_result`; failures as `carrier_error {carrier, state: timeout|error, code}`; `complete {per-carrier status OK|NO_SERVICE|TIMEOUT|ERROR, elapsed_ms, cached}` by 5 s; stream closes ≤ 6 s; heartbeat comment every 15 s (for the 30 s cap); client disconnect aborts outstanding calls (REQ-API-018). Non-streaming `GET /quotes/{id}` returns what has completed. **Cache (DR-B15):** Redis key = SHA-256 of the normalised request (origin, destination, packages in g/mm, content type, residential, ship date, account set, Mock scenario), value = **carrier cost responses only**, TTL 300 s; pricing/discounts/tax run after the cache so customer-specific prices are never shared; partial/failed responses are not cached; `cached: true` flag on results.

### Mock carrier (REQ-CARR-004..006)

Code `MOCK`, services `MOCK_EXPRESS|MOCK_STANDARD|MOCK_ECONOMY`, deterministic prices from billable weight × lane zone × service, scenario directives (DR-B29: `latency`, `fail`, `error`, `rates=empty`, `track=…`) from `X-Mock-Scenario` or a `MOCK:` customer reference; registered only when `NODE_ENV !== 'production'` (*superseded 2026-11-12 by amendment 9: explicit `MOCK_CARRIER_ENABLED` / `MOCK_SCENARIOS_ENABLED` flags*); absent from production fan-out and capability listings. Implements the full contract suite (REQ-CARR-011) — the proof that onboarding a carrier is adapter + capabilities + seed.

### Raw payload retention (REQ-CARR-018)

For `createShipment`, `cancelShipment`, `schedulePickup`, `cancelPickup` and every tracking event: request + response redacted (credentials, names, street lines, phone, email, tax IDs → `[REDACTED]`), stored as JSON objects in the `raw-carrier-payloads` bucket (ADR-0021) under `carrier/<code>/<shipment_id>/<operation>/<uuid>.json`, SHA-256 recorded in `carrier_raw_payloads`; never exposed to customers; staff access is M2.

### Carrier accounts (D-012)

`carrier_accounts` rows for `MOCK`, `DHL_EXPRESS`, `FEDEX`, `UPS` with `environment = sandbox`, `owner_type = PLATFORM`, `credential_secret_ref` (name only), `enabled` from config; config validation fails start-up if an enabled account lacks its secret reference (names reported, never values). Partner-owned accounts (M3) reuse the same table with `owner_org_id` and envelope-encrypted credentials (ADR-0011 seam).

## Alternatives considered

- **Per-carrier breaker instead of per-account.** Brief requires per account (sandbox and production accounts of one carrier fail independently).
- **Polling instead of SSE for progressive results.** More requests, worse first-result latency; SSE is required by the brief; the non-streaming GET exists for fallback.
- **Caching priced results.** Would leak customer-specific discounts; cache carrier costs only (REQ-CARR-010).
- **Third-party aggregator SDKs.** Would hide wire formats but add a dependency outside the fixed stack and licence/maintenance risk.

## Consequences

- Positive: one contract, four adapters, one test suite; failures are isolated per account; partial results always flow; secrets never leave the resolver.
- Negative: fixture realism is the main risk until credentials exist (R-01); DHL docs must be read manually at implementation (portal blocks bots).
- Verification: contract suite ×4 (NFR-QUAL-005), timeout/retry/breaker tests with Mock profiles (NFR-RES-001..003), k6 SSE scenarios (NFR-PERF-002/003), degradation e2e (NFR-RES-006).

## Amendments after the P3 security review (2026-11-12, `docs/05-quality/security/P3-architecture-security-review.md` SEC-P3-02, SEC-P3-08 item 2, SEC-P3-13; threat-model T-46, R-15, R-16, T-95; D-ARCH-018, D-ARCH-022, D-ARCH-026)

1. **Two bulkheads per carrier account (SEC-P3-02 item 1; supersedes the single `p-limit(10)` in §Resilience wrapper and the "bulkhead 10" value in §Context).** `rates` pool (`getRates`, `findServicePoints`, `validateAddress`, `estimateLandedCost`) and `transactional` pool (`createShipment`, `cancelShipment`, `schedulePickup`, `cancelPickup`, `uploadCustomsDocuments`, and `track` for booked shipments). Defaults `CARRIER_BULKHEAD_RATES=6`, `CARRIER_BULKHEAD_TRANSACTIONAL=4` per account (configuration; product-manager may tune, D-ARCH-004 pattern). Saga and tracking calls never queue behind rate shopping. The breaker stays one per account; a tripped breaker still fails both pools fast.
2. **Anonymous sub-budget (item 2; REQ-CARR-022, D-048).** Guests may hold at most `CARRIER_ANON_RATES_SHARE_PCT=50` % of the `rates` pool slots at any time (3 of 6). In addition, a **global anonymous token bucket per carrier account** (`CARRIER_ANON_CALLS_PER_MIN=120`, the product-manager default of D-048; Redis, shared across replicas) bounds anonymous calls across all IPs. When the share or the bucket is exhausted, guests receive an immediate `carrier_error {code: CARRIER_BUSY, state: error}` for that carrier or cached results only (DR-B15 cache) and security event `carrier_anon_budget_exhausted` is emitted (rate-limited to one per account per minute); authenticated quotes are unaffected. The per-IP `public-quote` class limits stand.
3. **Daily quota guard (item 3; REQ-CARR-023, D-048).** A Redis counter per account per UTC day (`CARRIER_DAILY_QUOTA`, per account; `0` = guard off, counter still metered — the default for Mock; 500 seeded for the DHL sandbox) with `CARRIER_QUOTA_RESERVE_PCT=40`. At (100 − reserve) % = 60 % anonymous fan-out to that account stops; at 90 % only `transactional` calls continue (authenticated rate shopping to that account also stops, the carrier is reported `status: ERROR, code: CARRIER_BUSY` in `complete`); alarms fire at 70 % and 90 % with security event `carrier_quota_pressure`. Transactional calls are never refused by the guard (the reserve exists for them).
4. **Saga semantics (item 4; ADR-0014 amendment 3).** `CARRIER_BUSY` (bulkhead queue wait beyond the deadline) and quota rejections are **local, no-network-I/O outcomes**: they are a distinct `LOCAL_REJECT` result class that is retryable and non-ambiguous. The booking saga retries them with backoff inside its 30-minute deadline and never moves a shipment to `DRAFT` or `EXCEPTION` because of them; `createShipment` is not "attempted" in the DR-B04 G2 sense when the call never left the process.
5. **Streams are subscribe-only (item 5; T-47).** `streamQuoteResults` never triggers fan-out; fan-out runs exactly once at `POST /quotes` and results are persisted (ADR-0016 amendment 3), so reopening a stream costs 0 carrier calls. Concurrent SSE streams are capped at `SSE_MAX_STREAMS_PER_IP=2` and `SSE_MAX_STREAMS_PER_USER=5` (both stream endpoints) → 429 `RATE_LIMITED`.
6. **Metrics (item 6).** `carrier_bulkhead_queue_depth{account,pool}`, `carrier_bulkhead_rejects_total{account,pool,class=anonymous|authenticated|transactional}`, `carrier_quota_usage_ratio{account}`, plus the existing breaker gauge; all feed `GET /carriers/status` and the alerts in ADR-0017. Verification: SEC-TC-26 (amended), SEC-TC-40, SEC-TC-41, SEC-TC-42; NFR-RES-003 row updated; the measurable isolation target is the new NFR-RES-007 (D-048; story US-293). Residual: MEDIUM — distributed floods can still deny **guests** rate shopping until the M5 WAF (RR-03), never paying customers.
7. **Carrier OAuth token cache is encrypted (SEC-P3-08 item 2, R-16; amends §Ports `TokenCache`).** Tokens cached in Redis (`carrier:token:<account_id>`, TTL = `expires_in − 60 s`) are AES-256-GCM ciphertext under `CARRIER_TOKEN_CACHE_KEY` with AAD = `carrier_token ‖ account_id ‖ key_id` (key register, ADR-0018 amendment 1); `CARRIER_TOKEN_CACHE=memory` keeps them in process memory only. Redis access uses per-service Valkey ACL users (`api`, `worker`) with key-prefix limits locally as well as in AWS (platform.md §2.2, §4.3).
8. **Raw payload redaction covers blobs and free text (SEC-P3-13, T-95; amends §Raw payload retention).** (a) The redactor replaces every base64 or binary document field (labels, PDFs, paperless uploads, in requests and responses of `createShipment`, `uploadCustomsDocuments` and any other operation) with `{"$blob": {"sha256", "size", "media_type", "document_id"?}}`; the bytes themselves live only in the `documents` bucket under the ADR-0019/0021 rules. (b) Redaction of **tracking payloads and all responses** is **allow-list based**: canonical status codes, timestamps, carrier references and tracking numbers, city and country codes, service and surcharge codes, amounts and currencies are kept; every other string (signatory names, free-text locations, remarks) becomes `[REDACTED]`. Request redaction keeps the existing field-name deny-list plus the blob rule. (c) A property test feeds synthetic PII and an embedded PDF containing sentinel text through the redactor and asserts that no sentinel, name, street line, phone, e-mail, tax ID or base64 run > 1 kB survives (SEC-TC-52 raw-payload part). REQ-CARR-018 wording aligned by product-manager (D-052 b; closes R-29).
9. **Mock gating by explicit flags; carrier pushes are hints (SEC-P3-21 items 1 and 4, LOW; aligns with REQ-CARR-006 as reworded in D-052 e; amends §Mock carrier).** The Mock carrier is registered only when `MOCK_CARRIER_ENABLED=true` and scenario directives are honoured only when `MOCK_SCENARIOS_ENABLED=true`; both default to `true` only for `APP_ENV ∈ {local, test}`, are `false` otherwise, and `APP_ENV=production` refuses to start with either set (`NODE_ENV` is no longer the gate). The edge strips `X-Mock-Scenario` in non-local environments (platform.md; devops-engineer in Wave 0). Carrier **push** events (REQ-TRACK-006) are hints: a push that would trigger a cancellation-blocking or terminal transition is applied only after confirmation by `track()` for that shipment (T-72); REQ-TRACK-002's forward-only rule still applies to both.
10. **Authenticated and unverified callers are budgeted too (SEC-P3-29, MEDIUM; residual of SEC-P3-02; completes amendments 2–3; D-ARCH-031).** Registration is free and `PENDING_VERIFICATION` users may quote, so one account at the 600/min `authenticated` rate could push a carrier's daily quota to 90 % in about a minute and stop rate shopping to that carrier for every customer until 00:00 UTC. Paid bookings were already protected by the reserve; this closes the remaining denial path.
    1. **Unverified users count as anonymous** for the whole carrier budget — the anonymous `rates` share (≤ 50 %), the global anonymous token bucket (`CARRIER_ANON_CALLS_PER_MIN`) and the 60 % quota stop of amendment 3 — and for the per-IP `public-quote` HTTP class. `users.email_verified_at IS NOT NULL` is the only way out (ADR-0006 amendment 15 expires unverified accounts after 7 days).
    2. **`quote-create` rate class** (ADR-0016 amendment 11) for verified authenticated callers of `createQuote`, replacing the 600/min `authenticated` class on that route: `RATE_QUOTE_CREATE_PER_USER_PER_MIN=30`, `RATE_QUOTE_CREATE_PER_USER_PER_HOUR=300`, `RATE_QUOTE_CREATE_PER_ORG_PER_HOUR=1000` (configuration; product-manager may tune, D-ARCH-004 pattern) → 429 `RATE_LIMITED`. 30 quotes a minute is far above any interactive use and still two orders of magnitude below the drain rate.
    3. **Per-organisation quota share.** Each organisation may consume at most `CARRIER_QUOTA_ORG_SHARE_PCT=10` % of a carrier account's `CARRIER_DAILY_QUOTA` through rates-pool calls (Redis counter per account × organisation × UTC day, metered even when the guard is off). Beyond it, that organisation's fan-out to the account returns `carrier_error {code: CARRIER_BUSY, state: error}` or DR-B15 cached results, every other organisation is unaffected, and the security event `carrier_quota_org_share_exhausted` fires at most once per account and organisation per minute. Transactional calls are never refused by the share (the reserve of amendment 3 protects them). Metric `carrier_quota_org_share_exhausted_total{account}` joins amendment 6 (no organisation label, to keep cardinality bounded; the organisation is in the event).
    4. Requirement side (product-manager, in parallel): REQ-CARR-022/023 and DR-A22 carry the values; US-293 gains the three scenarios of SEC-TC-62. Verification: SEC-TC-62; NFR-RES-007 row updated in `nfr-strategy.md`/`nfr-traceability.md`. Residual after this amendment: RR-03 returns to guests only (distributed anonymous floods until the M5 WAF).

## Amendment (2026-11-12, SPK-02): carrier documentation capture results (TASK-009, PR #6 @4e5d05f; `docs/04-plan/spikes/SPK-02.md` §2 and §4; `rulings/wave-0-batch-3-rulings.md` item 1; D-ARCH-057)

The PLAN §9 fallback applies: those operations are built from the READMEs with fixtures flagged `synthesised`, and the live sandbox run (`CARRIER_LIVE_SANDBOX=1`, COND-SANDBOX-DEMO) remains the closing proof. 11. **Results, decisions and rules.**
    1. **Authoritative status.** SPK-02 §1 (evidence per carrier) and each README's "ADR-0010 re-verify items: result" section are the record of what is confirmed, confirmed (secondary) or unverified. The corrections of SPK-02 §2 are adopted into this ADR: UPS void is `DELETE /shipments/v2409/void/cancel/{id}` (`trackingnumber` as a query parameter); UPS label recovery exists (`POST /labels/v1/recovery`) and is the path to recover a label after a failed download (guardrail: zero duplicate labels); UPS Address Validation is `v2`, Locator `v3`, Paperless `v2`, Landed Cost `v1`; UPS token responses are **strings** (`expires_in`, `issued_at`) and the token path `/security/v1/oauth/token` sits **outside** the `/api` prefix of every other operation; DHL tracking has a single form (`GET /shipments/{n}/tracking`) and a batch form (`GET /tracking`, ≤ 200 numbers, no per-event GMT offsets); FedEx may return **HTTP 200 with a non-empty `errors[]`** — the adapter checks the body on success; FedEx limits are 1,400 transactions per 10 s per project (429 "Retry after 10 seconds", honour `Retry-After` else 10 s) and the token service penalises a public IP with a 10-minute 403 — the single in-flight refresh per account (§Ports) is therefore mandatory, not an optimisation. Capability seeds: `pushTracking = false` for all three; `maxTrackingNumbersPerCall` UPS 1, FedEx 30, DHL 200 (`carriers.max_tracking_numbers_per_call`).
    2. **DHL Express `cancelShipment` is declared unsupported.** No cancel-shipment operation exists in any readable MyDHL source (the third-party SDK wraps every other operation; pickup cancellation exists). The `CarrierAdapter` interface keeps `cancelShipment` as a method (brief §4.3, REQ-CARR-001), but `capabilities()` gains `operations.cancelShipment: boolean`, and the REQ-CARR-003 guard (`CAPABILITY_NOT_SUPPORTED`, **no network I/O**) now also covers this one required operation; the DHL adapter declares `false` until the official specification shows an operation, at which point the flag flips and the method is implemented (reversibility high). **Cancellation semantics when the carrier cannot void (REQ-BOOK-029, REQ-DOC-013, REQ-PICK-008, D-017 unchanged):** the shipment moves to `CANCELLED`; `cancelPickup` is still called when a pickup exists; the carrier-void step records `carrier_void = UNSUPPORTED` (a terminal, non-failed outcome — never `EXCEPTION`, never a retry); the label document is marked `VOIDED` **Platform-side** at once (REQ-DOC-014's 410 then applies) and the refund proceeds. The saga's audit entry carries `details.carrier_void = 'UNSUPPORTED'`; no raw payload exists for the skipped call. Assumption recorded for the first live run: an Express shipment that is never handed over is not invoiced by DHL (verify at COND-SANDBOX-DEMO). The contract suite (REQ-CARR-011, TASK-101) gains the case `cancel-capability-not-supported-no-io`, executed against every adapter that declares `false` (DHL) and the Mock with a scenario that declares it. **Demo-critical scope is not affected:** US-196, US-216, US-229 and US-261 are Should and not DC; US-237 (Must, not DC) keeps working because DHL pickup cancellation exists. product-manager mirrors the semantics in REQ-BOOK-029/REQ-DOC-013 ("void the carrier shipment **where the adapter supports it**; otherwise Platform-side void") and REQ-CARR-003 ("an operation the adapter declares unsupported").
    3. **UPS PDF labels come from the official Label Recovery API; a new optional operation `recoverLabel`.** Verified in the pinned official specification (`UPS-API/api-documentation@fd57ca9`, `Shipping.yaml:11251-11263` and `:13479-13496`, read 2026-11-12): the **Ship** `LabelImageFormat.Code` admits `GIF`, `ZPL`, `EPL`, `SPL` only, while **Label Recovery** `LabelImageFormat.Code` admits `GIF`, **`PDF`**, `ZPL`, `EPL`, `SPL` ("PDF -- label is in PDF format"). The Platform stores labels only in `document_format` values (`PDF_4X6`, `PDF_A4`, `PDF_LETTER`, `ZPL`; REQ-DOC-002/015); raster carrier output is never a document. Design: (a) `CarrierAdapter` gains the **optional** operation `recoverLabel(ref: CarrierShipmentRef, formats: LabelFormat[], ctx): Promise<LabelDocument[]>` — idempotent, retryable (`getRates`-class retry policy), `transactional` pool, raw payload retained as part of the shipment's record, capability flag `operations.recoverLabel` (UPS and Mock `true`; FedEx and DHL `false` → `CAPABILITY_NOT_SUPPORTED`). (b) The UPS adapter's `createShipment` requests `GIF` (or `ZPL` for thermal services), returns `labels: []` for the PDF formats (the GIF survives only as a `{"$blob"}` in the raw payload, amendment 8, and is never offered to users) and declares `labelFormats = [PDF_4X6, ZPL]` for its services — `PDF_4X6` only, since Label Recovery renders the 4 × 6 label and REQ-DOC-002 offers "only that format" when the carrier returns one. (c) The booking saga's document step (ADR-0014 step 4, `BOOKED → LABEL_READY`, retry-only) asks the carrier port for every offered format missing from `createShipment`'s result through `recoverLabel` (`POST /labels/v1/recovery`, `LabelImageFormat.Code = PDF`, shipment identifier only); the same operation serves the "label download failed → recover, never re-create" guardrail (zero duplicate labels). Recovery failures follow step 4's existing retry and exception path (the shipment stays `BOOKED`, an operator alert is raised); they never trigger a second `createShipment`. (d) Cost: one extra `transactional` call per UPS booking in the worker, off the customer's critical path; UPS quota metered (`CARRIER_DAILY_QUOTA = 0`).     5. **No hard-coded token lifetime; token-service failure handling (PR6-SEC-05, PR6-SEC-07; amends §Ports `TokenCache`).** `TokenCache` derives the TTL from each token response: `ttl = clamp(Number(expires_in) − CARRIER_TOKEN_REFRESH_SKEW_S, 0, CARRIER_TOKEN_TTL_MAX_S)` with defaults `60` and `3600` (configuration keys, ADR-0018 §6; owner TASK-131). (c) **a token-endpoint 403 is handled by a static per-adapter policy, not by the status code alone** *(revised 2026-11-12, PR6-SEC-11, D-ARCH-067 — the first text applied FedEx's per-IP penalty to every carrier, so one blocked UPS account would have stopped token requests for all UPS accounts for 600 s and re-triggered on every probe)*: each OAuth adapter exports `TOKEN_ENDPOINT_POLICY: { on403: 'carrier-cooldown' | 'account-negative-cache' }` next to `EGRESS`. **FedEx** `'carrier-cooldown'`: the token service penalises the shared public IP, so a 403 sets the carrier-wide cool-down `CARRIER_TOKEN_PENALTY_COOLDOWN_S = 600` for all FedEx accounts on that egress (fail fast `CARRIER_UNAVAILABLE`, no retries, one probe after the cool-down, security/operations event `carrier_token_penalty`). **UPS** `'account-negative-cache'`: a 403 means "Blocked Merchant" — one account blocked or not enabled for the product — so it sets that account's negative cache with `CARRIER_TOKEN_BLOCKED_TTL_S = 600` (fail fast `AUTH_FAILED`), opens that account's breaker and raises an operations event; **other UPS accounts keep working** and no carrier-wide key is written. **DHL Express** authenticates with HTTP Basic per call, has no token endpoint and declares no policy. ## Amendment (2026-11-20, TASK-041 security review PR041-SEC-03; D-ARCH-178)

13. **The carrier admission controls never fail open (completes amendments 2, 3 and 10).** The anonymous token bucket, the daily quota guard and the organisation share keep their counters in Valkey. Amendments 2 and 3 did not say what happens when a counter cannot be read or written (an error, a timeout, `OOM` under `noeviction`, an open breaker). The HTTP classes in front of them partly fail open (ADR-0016 amendment 12 item 8), so without a rule a Valkey outage would become unbounded anonymous carrier traffic.
    1. **Refuse what the control can refuse.** While the counters are unavailable, anonymous fan-out to the account (guests and unverified users) is refused locally, and so are rates-pool calls of verified callers to an account whose `CARRIER_DAILY_QUOTA` is above 0. The outcome is the `LOCAL_REJECT` class of amendment 4: `CARRIER_BUSY` for that carrier, cached results still served, the quote still created. An account with the guard off (`CARRIER_DAILY_QUOTA=0`, the Mock default) keeps serving verified callers.
    2. **Never refuse a booking.** `transactional` calls continue, as amendment 3 says. They are counted in process and added to the day's counter when the store answers again. If that addition is lost, the counter is short by the bookings of the outage at most.
    3. **No waiting.** The store calls are bounded by 250 ms and sit behind a breaker, as in ADR-0016 amendment 12 item 7, so a refusal costs no wait.
    4. **Signal.** The runtime security event `rate_limit_store_unavailable` with `scope: carrier-admission`, at most once a minute per instance; the refusals count in `carrier_bulkhead_rejects_total`.
    5. **Why refusal and not a per-instance fallback.** K replicas would multiply the anonymous bucket by K during the one period when the shared view is missing. What it risks is slow to recover: a carrier's daily quota is gone until 00:00 UTC (amendment 10), and carrier-side throttling is keyed by the egress IP that every replica shares (amendment 11 records a 10-minute FedEx penalty for its token service). Guests and rate shopping are the traffic the platform can pause; bookings are not.
    6. **Owner and proof.** TASK-190, which builds the bucket and the guard. Tests: with the store failing, a guest quote gets `CARRIER_BUSY` for the account and makes no carrier call; a booking still reaches the carrier; the calls of the outage are added to the counter afterwards. SEC-TC-41 gains the outage row (qa-lead). Until TASK-190 the only carrier is the Mock, and the per-instance fallback of the `public-quote` and `public-tracking` classes is the guard.
