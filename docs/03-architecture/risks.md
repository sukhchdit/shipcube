| R-ARCH-37 | **The per-aggregate order of outbox events rests on a convention of the publisher and ends at the enqueue** (TASK-043 code review M1; ADR-0004 amendment 2 items 13 to 15; D-ARCH-185). (a) The relay orders by the insert time of the event. That is the order of the business changes only when the publishing transaction holds the row lock of the aggregate at that moment. A handler that publishes before its write, or that never locks the aggregate row, can have its event enqueued before the event of an earlier change, and nothing reports it; the writer cannot check the rule. (b) BullMQ does not keep the enqueue order through processing (`outbox-relay` runs 5 jobs at a time and retries after a delay), so a handler that relied on order would act on a stale sequence. The per-aggregate consumer lock that ADR-0004 item 3 sketched is not built, and a lock with retry gives mutual exclusion, not order. (c) *Added 2026-11-20 (D-QG-002):* the order key is the wall clock of the primary. A step backwards that is larger than the time between two commits on one aggregate would invert them. It is not mitigated; after a failover that time is tens of seconds | L | M | (a) The natural code order, write then publish, meets the rule.
